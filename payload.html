Tree Identification Notes

These notes cover a few common ways to identify a tree without damaging it.

Leaves

Leaf shape is the first clue. Opposite leaves grow in pairs on the twig; alternate leaves grow one at a time. Compound leaves have several leaflets on one stem, while simple leaves have a single blade. Margins can be smooth, toothed, or lobed.

Bark

Bark changes with age. Young birch is smooth and pale; older birch peels in papery strips. Oak bark is deeply furrowed. Beech stays smooth and gray even when mature. Note the color, texture, and any lenticels.

Twigs and buds

Twigs carry buds in patterns that match the leaves. Look at bud shape, color, and whether the buds sit opposite or alternate. A hand lens helps with small buds and leaf scars.

Site

Soil, water, and light narrow the list. A tree in a wet hollow is a different candidate from one on a dry ridge. Record the setting before comparing species.
